People differ in where their interest habitually flows: outward to the object, or inward to the subject. They also differ in which of four functions they trust most: thinking, feeling, sensation or intuition. Every psychology is built by someone of a particular type, and it becomes false the moment it claims to describe everyone. The favoured function grows strong. The others stay crude and act on us from below, and the unconscious keeps pushing the neglected opposite forward until, under enough strain, it breaks through. Growth does not come from perfecting the strong side. It comes from accepting the weak side through a living symbol that holds both, which slowly makes a person into an individual.

## Every psychology carries its maker

The observer's own psychology shapes observation, interpretation and theory. We see colours, not wave-lengths. An extravert necessarily builds a different psychology from an introvert: one makes observable facts absolute, the other insists on the reality of the idea. Freud's empirical bias toward detailed facts is one such psychology, and each tendency needs the other as a corrective.

The standpoint decides the reading. A lily can be a chance grouping of organic matter or a symbol of purity, and both readings are rational. A biased view can still catch something real in another person, since the beam in your own eye helps you find your brother's mote. It goes wrong when it becomes a general theory that all motes are beams. A typologist who paints the opposite type pitilessly and their own type kindly is showing their own shadow, not the facts. **Any description of a psyche must be corrected for the type of the person who wrote it.**

Since theories come from their author's psyche, no formula can serve as a universal therapy.

> The assumption that there exists only one psychology or only one fundamental psychological principle is an intolerable tyranny, belonging to the pseudoscientific prejudice of the normal man.

The same holds for society. Equality before the law is right. Equal external conditions are another matter, because the same income or opportunity means much to one person and little to another, especially to someone whose greatest possibility lies within. Every general external form will be unjust to some type.

A theory built on one drive, such as power or sexuality, explains only secondary features of people whose highest value lies elsewhere. At the unconscious foundation people are alike: a Black man in the American South can dream in motifs from Greek mythology. Their conscious minds, though, differ beyond measure. A uniform conscious psyche is a lecturer's convenience.

We all lean to one side, so we read others through our own type. The introvert traces the extravert's actions to hidden subjective motives. The extravert treats the introvert's inner life as a product of circumstance or as mere fantasy. Disputes between rational people usually come from different premises, weighted toward subject or object, yet both sides hunt for a fallacy in the other's conclusion. Small aversions working unnoticed in the background colour even fair-minded reasoning. In small matters goodwill bridges the gap. Where the ideal of a type is at stake, understanding is usually impossible.

This has practical weight. A collectively minded person can understand others only by projection, which is why earlier ages barely studied individual differences. A patient unconsciously takes on or rejects the analyst's whole outlook through the transference, and ignoring this is like a surgeon ignoring hemorrhage. So the analyst must have been analysed and must have faced their own life problems first. More generally, judging another psyche accurately requires knowing your own and having freed yourself from collective opinion.

Freud's theory is extraverted: the key is the subject's sexual relation to the object, and repression blocks the way to it. Adler's is introverted: the key is the ego's power and its defensive isolation from the object. Each is true for its own type. Fantasies stand between ego and instinct and contain both, so either reading forces the material. Analysis guided by the analyst's type only deepens the patient's one-sidedness, because whatever surfaces from the neglected functions gets explained away and repressed again.

Psychic facts look both backward and forward. Causal reduction is legitimate but too cheap for significant events. Tracing Saul's vision to envy of Christ leaves you empty-handed; reading it as his unconscious moving toward the Pauline mission explains more. Reduction treats a dream image as a sign of an elementary wish and dissolves its meaning. The constructive method treats it as a symbolic anticipation of where the person is heading. The two methods complement each other, and synthesis leads to a new attitude, called the transcendent function.

There are two realities, outer and inner, and neurosis is a failure to adapt to both. Snakes seen in delirium are unreal objectively and wholly real to the patient. The genuine neurotic must first obey the law of their own being; only then can they meet traditional demands and pay Caesar. A psychological formula that claims to explain life puts a ready-made answer between a person and their real problems, which makes it destructive. A principle, by contrast, simply renders a living process in a form we can think about.

The aim is individuation: consciously separating your individuality from collective psychology. Individuality is given from birth but exists psychologically only once it is made conscious. Until then it is projected onto objects, which then carry too much power. A plant must grow in its soil before it can unfold its own character, so basic collective adaptation comes first. Done properly, individuation deepens solidarity, because a society of deformed individuals cannot stay healthy. The individual way is never a norm and is not truly opposed to the norm. Norms are orientation drawn from many individual ways. A life shaped entirely by the collective norm is the less moral for it.


## Two directions and four functions

Extraversion is interest moving out toward the object; introversion is interest withdrawing toward the subject. They pulse like the heart's contraction and expansion. The extravert affirms the object, thinks and acts in clear dependence on it, and can even be pulled away from themselves by it. The introvert's stance is abstracting, as if the object's hold must always be checked, and the object becomes secondary or a token of an idea. Either movement is active when chosen and passive when compelled. Introversion can be intellectual, emotional, intuitive or sensory.

Everyone has both mechanisms.

> Hence, there can never occur a pure type in the sense that he is entirely possessed of the one mechanism with a complete atrophy of the other.

A type forms when one mechanism becomes chronically dominant. Inborn gift and the childhood setting both push one way, since some parents demand restraint and others sympathy, and development follows the line of least resistance. Milieus also harden attitudes: hence the person who is an angel abroad and a devil at home. Attitude is a readiness to act in a definite direction. It selects what you perceive and what you miss. It even bends general laws: one person can do without sex harmlessly while another suffers at slight deprivation.

Attitude types are distinct from function types. Within each attitude, people differ by which of four basic, irreducible functions leads. It is tempting to equate thinking with introversion and feeling with extraversion, and also to equate types with activity or passivity. Both equations are wrong. Any function can be introverted or extraverted, so there are eight types.

1. **Thinking links presentations through concepts by judgment.** Stringing associations together is mere presentation, not thinking. Directed thinking is willed and rational; passive thinking happens to you and is irrational. Thinking governed by feeling keeps logic only as a facade.
2. **Feeling assigns value by acceptance or rejection.** It is as rational as thinking, because values are given according to reason. Active feeling is loving; passive feeling is being in love. The intellect can classify feelings but never capture what feeling is.
3. **Sensation is conscious perception of what is there.** Concrete sensation of a flower brings in stem, scent and botany at once. Abstract, aesthetic sensation picks out only its brilliant red.
4. **Intuition is perception through the unconscious.** It delivers a whole with certainty and no visible route, which is why Spinoza ranked intuitive knowledge highest. It can be subjective or objective, concrete or abstract.

"Irrational" means outside reason, not against it. That the earth has a moon is an irrational fact. Reason is not a cosmic principle. It is human adaptation to average events, deposited over ages, much as the eye evolved to fit light. **Some psychological conflicts have only irrational solutions, and insisting on a rational one can block the real answer.** In rational types the perceiving functions are pushed down, so their sensations and intuitions come out primitive: compulsive pleasure-seeking, or suspicion that smells out everything ugly.

Sensation and intuition types act on the intensity of what they perceive. They are deeply empirical, so their judgment cannot keep pace with their experience. Their unconscious judgments come out as crude, cold criticisms that look calculating to rational observers. Each kind finds the other half alive. Sensation and intuition compensate each other as thinking and feeling do, and together they are the soil from which thinking and feeling grow. Sensation predominates in children and in primitive peoples.

Every pronounced type tends to compensate for its one-sidedness, which keeps the psyche in balance but makes types hard to see, especially in yourself. The type descriptions hold for conscious psychology only, because in the unconscious the relations reverse. Consciousness is selective, like an eye with a narrow focus. What it excludes forms a counterweight, and the more one-sided the conscious attitude, the more opposed the contents that rise. The oppressed person who scents oppression everywhere is unconsciously striving for power. This self-regulation is broader than Adler's balancing of inferiority. Neurosis disrupts it, and therapy restores it by making unconscious contents conscious.

When an extreme tendency dominates long enough, its opposite emerges over time. This is enantiodromia: Saul becoming Paul, Nietzsche turning from deifying Wagner to hating him.

Type is innate. Two children of the same mother can show opposite types early, and types are scattered across classes, sexes and siblings, which rules out conscious choice. The two attitudes mirror nature's two strategies: many offspring with little protection, or few offspring well defended. Blake called them the prolific and the devouring. A child forced by an abnormal parent into the opposite type usually becomes neurotic later, and the cure lies in developing the natural attitude.

Society rewards the function you are best at, so you cultivate it and identify with it. That makes you collectively adapted and socially successful, and it costs the neglected parts of you. These inferior functions are undeveloped, not diseased. Starved of energy, they regress to archaic forms and disturb consciousness, and the person strains the favoured function harder until collapse. Identification is unconscious imitation, as when a son lives his father's ways as though he were the father. It helps development early and becomes pathological once your own path is open. Still, identifying with one function is a necessary stage toward individuation. A function becomes capable of direction only once it is differentiated from the others.

An undeveloped function acts autonomously. Schiller complained to Goethe that the poet overtook him whenever he tried to philosophize, and the philosopher whenever he tried to write poems, because his undifferentiated feeling fed both. Such functions appear in dreams as fighting animals or monsters. Their archaism shows as fusion of functions, ambivalence, and concretism, the superstitious overvaluation of facts behind slogans like "Man is what he eats." A neglected function keeps its energy, which activates archaic fantasies, and the function can be freed only by bringing those fantasies to consciousness.

Beneath the personal unconscious of forgotten and repressed material lies an inherited collective unconscious. The newborn's lungs know how to breathe because the body carries inherited functional experience, and the psyche carries the same. Its primordial images, or archetypes, are deposits of countless repeated experiences, without content yet shaping all experience, much as Kant's categories shape thought. Even fantasy cannot roam infinitely. The archetype is the counterpart of instinct, supplying the grasp of a situation that action needs. An idea is the meaning of such an image detached from its concreteness. The psyche meets nature with a symbol as the organism meets light with an eye, and that symbol mediates between a formulated idea and crude feeling.


## The early Church and the sacrifice of the best function

The Gnostic division into Pneumatici, Psychici and Hylici may correspond to thinking, feeling and sensation. Tertullian was an introverted thinker with a fierce intellect flanked by sensuality. The intellect tied Tertullian to the world, so that intellect was sacrificed: the Son of God's death is credible because it is absurd. That sacrifice opened a depth of religious feeling. Origen was an extravert devoted to the object, so Origen's danger lay in sensual ties, and the sacrifice was castration, which left the way open to absorb Gnostic learning.

> Tertullian, the acute thinker, becomes the man of feeling, while Origen becomes the scholar and loses himself in the intellect.

**A one-sided attitude leaves a deficit that accumulates until it forces the sacrifice of the favoured function, and the type reverses.** No single function grasps an object whole, and the accumulated lack is felt as vague dissatisfaction. This is the mechanism of conversion. The Church's narrow rule of faith hurt intellectuals most, which helps explain the pull of Gnosticism. That concretistic age projected its inner change of attitude outward as cosmogony and took the figures for real.

The Christological fights follow the same line. Ebionites saw Christ as purely human, Docetists as mere appearance; Homoiousia stressed the humanly perceptible, Homoousia the abstract concept. For the crowd these were slogans for power struggles; for the founders they were real. In the Pelagian dispute Augustine's original sin devalued man and exalted the Church, while Pelagius asserted moral freedom. Each standpoint denies the other's chief value, and the more firmly people identify with their own, the more they violate the other's, often with the best intentions.

Scotus Erigena set reason above authority and read communion as commemoration; Erigena's own monks murdered the abbot. Radbertus gave the age the concrete miracle of transubstantiation, which it craved, and won. An absurd dogma can still serve development, since belief in the miracle helped free thought from the purely sensuous. Later Luther held to the real presence because its sensuous feeling-value was indispensable, while Zwingli kept the rational, symbolic view. The two standpoints exclude each other.


## Names, ideas and the third standpoint of fantasy

The medieval quarrel over universals had three positions: universals before things (Platonic realism), after things (nominalism, mere names), and in things (Aristotle's common-sense middle). Nominalism equates reality with individual things; realism places reality in the idea. The extravert, held by particulars, is a natural nominalist. The introvert, for whom ideas are always in the foreground, is a natural realist. Their thinking is incommensurable: the introvert's is determined by reason, the extravert's by a feel for what the age will accept. Historical figures cannot be typed confidently on scant evidence.

Treating ideas as real is a survival of the primitive mind, for which images are as vivid as perceptions. A dead mother is seen and heard as a ghost. Belief in spirits grows from this, not from a need for explanation. Even educated patients must be reminded that thinking is not acting. Mystics deliberately revive the inner image to counterweigh extraversion, and scholastics called mental contents "res" alongside outer things. Abstraction loses thing-ness as it climbs: warm things, warmth, temperature, energy.

Sophistic puzzles like the veiled man showed that word and thing are not magically one, a real gain. Yet general concepts name real likenesses. **The idea of energy is a verbal concept, and the electric company still pays dividends on it.** The nominalist is no closer to things, since the word "grass" is not grass either. Extreme nominalism ends up wishing for some vague all-oneness. Universal ideas are among civilization's highest values, and tearing them down leads back toward brutality. When thinking serves personal feeling, in either type, it becomes negatively critical and reduces everything to the singular; when the thought process itself carries the value, the idea rises above the many. Reducing universal values to personal motives is pseudo-psychology.

The ontological argument proves nothing; Kant showed a hundred real dollars contain no more than a hundred possible ones. It survives because for some people an idea is as real as the world, and the stubborn power of the word points to something psychologically real. Between intellect and thing stands the psyche: being in the soul makes the argument unnecessary. Psychologically, a person's God is the complex holding most of their energy, which may be their belly, money, science, power or sex.

Abélard tried to unite feeling-into, which stresses each thing's difference, with abstraction, which stresses likeness. Consistent realism or nominalism gives clarity; the middle gives confusion and satisfies nobody, so some pupils took Abélard for a nominalist and others for a realist. Because the truth of one type is the error of the other, the attempt matters. Each type compensates inside its own frame: the idea-man turns passionate, the object-man turns logician. But the attempt failed, being purely intellectual. Psychology is the mediating science that could unite idea and thing. Any purely intellectual formula of reconciliation must end in paradox and lose the living object, and since this split is the daily problem of how a person relates to self and world, a third standpoint is needed.

That standpoint is creative fantasy.

> Each new day reality is created by the psyche. The only expression I can use for this activity is phantasy.

Fantasy is feeling, thought, intuition and sensation at once. It bridges subject and object, gives perceptions their intensity and ideas their force, and is the mother of both thinking and feeling. Its principle is play, and no creative work was ever born without it, so dismissing imagination as infantile is short-sighted. Schiller's play instinct is this activity. Creation is play from inner necessity, which looks like play only from outside. Fantasy needs limits, set by reality and necessity, not by intellect. Truth is a programme, not an eternity; the more eternal a truth, the emptier.

Engagement with fantasy varies inversely with an age's rationalism. Completed religions suppress individual fantasy and substitute fixed symbols drawn from the founder's revelations; Jeremiah condemned prophets who told their own dreams. What separates a founder from a madman is refusing to take the fantasy concretely: Christ read the devil's offer of earthly kingship as a heavenly one. The desert monks' devils were their own unconscious protesting, and they were taught to reject them even when they spoke truly. Suppression withdraws energy that then builds consciousness, which explains modern hostility to the unconscious. Science must exclude fantasy, but science must also serve life, and a practical psychology makes the intellect a tool of creative fantasy. Fantasies carry the repressed opposite attitude, attach to the inferior function, and first release crude impulses, not a love of beauty.


## Culture's wound and the living way between opposites

Culture wounded modern people by developing one function at the expense of the rest. Greek individual culture rested on slavery; collective culture was Christianity's achievement. Once every soul counted, slavery moved inward. The superior function now rules the enslaved inferior ones inside each person, an open wound like that of Amfortas. Large organizations use only the preferred function, so a person sinks to that function and a gulf opens between what they are and what they represent. No culture swinging to one side, collective or individual, is complete.

This was necessary: concentrating energy in one faculty achieves what nature alone cannot. But it must provoke a reaction. The function culture develops is the one heredity already favours, and greater individual values may lie in the neglected ones. Christianity offered redemption by detaching one valued function. The present task is to accept the inferior functions, which oppose the superior one only in their neglected form.

1. **Release the energy of the inferior functions into differentiation.** Wishes and precepts cannot do it; a potential must be created that lets latent energy flow.
2. **Expect a lower but balanced level.** A lower energy can be raised only at a higher one's expense, so neither function regains the superior's original value. Trying to convert the inferior function directly is building a perpetual motion machine.
3. **Accept that the conflict will be civil war.** Admitting the repressed functions unlocks opposites, and settling them by force sacrifices freedom, as the French Revolution ended in Napoleon.

Barbarism is one-sidedness, including the worship of reason. The goddess of reason was powerless before the Terror. Deifying the superior function and sensual excess are the same untamed energy. Seeing a personal conflict mirrored in public events lends it collective force; the French Revolution likely gave Schiller courage. The force crushing individuality is no new institution but an archaic collective mentality, participation mystique, within us. Idealizing a golden past is a pagan habit, and beauty needs its opposite, since aesthetic flowering often comes with political decline. **The ideal Greek should be read as the archaic, natural person inside us, rejected by collective consciousness yet the bearer of the beauty we seek.**

Nature and culture admit no rational compromise. Rousseau saw you must choose between making a man or a citizen. A way must be found that the living being can actually walk, because wise precepts have existed since Pythagoras and never sufficed. The greatest changes came by routes contemporaries called absurd, so Schiller's philosophical formulas must be read as symbols. Opposites cannot be united rationally: there is no third term in logic. They are reconciled only through a new thing that arises in living.

When an obstacle dams up energy, the united opposites split and fight until exhausted, and from their energy a third element begins the new way. A struggle with an outer object becomes inner, since striving to master it identifies us partly with it; the affects that follow let us watch the opposites. The introvert locates the person in the constant self and favours an eternal God; the extravert finds themself in changing relations and favours a God who becomes human.

The introvert's abstracting stance, turning every event into experience and every experience into law, brings poverty. Schiller told Goethe that Goethe had a kingdom to rule and Schiller only a family of ideas. Restricting the object consciously breeds an unconscious, compulsive, sensual craving for it, so Schiller saw the senses as the road to the divine. Sensuous feeling ties the introvert to objects through participation mystique, which drives more abstraction in a vicious circle. The formula "externalize everything within and shape everything without" risks violence unless the inner world is rich. The introverted thinker experiences the sensuous instinct as passive sensation and fuses undeveloped feeling and sensation. Lapsing into this archaic tie is not real extraversion. Lofty conscious god-likeness breeds an unconscious god-likeness aimed down toward a sensual, brutal god, which eventually surfaces.

Each function grasps only part of reality, so thinking commands at most a third or a fourth of it. Exalting it is like having good eyes and being deaf. Differentiated feeling can establish universal values too; only crude feeling is bound to the case. The inferior side feels alien and destructive to its owner, though for the opposite type it is their real nature. The opposition of the two instincts is life-promoting and should be kept. Giving up the claim that one function is universally valid leads to individualism, realizing a person as they are.

There is a separable individual core that is neither sensuousness nor reason; without it, the person dissolves into the opposites or crushes one. The will cannot unite them, since its content always comes from one side. Consciousness is discrimination and cannot either. The reconciling content must come from the unconscious, where opposites are still fused. Its contents are weakly charged, so they blur and substitute for one another, which gives them their mediating, symbolic character. Normally the symbol stays below the threshold; when unconscious values outweigh conscious ones, the result is mental disorder. A symbol is both real and unreal, so neither the rational nor the sensuous function can make one. Recognizing projection clears false glamour from objects. Ancient demons were unconscious complexes breaking in, arising when a person takes one side and the unconscious takes the other.

Withdraw energy from both outer objects and inner ideas into the self, and it sinks into the unconscious, animates waiting fantasy, and brings up symbols that show the way. This collaboration of conscious and unconscious around a living symbol is the transcendent function. It is nothing mystical; "transcendent" means only that it allows a transition from one attitude to another. The ego's steadfastness and the symbol's superiority condition each other.

> The raw material, when elaborated by the thesis and antithesis, which in its process of formation reconciles the opposites, is the living symbol.

If one side captures the symbol, the split recurs on a higher level. If the ego holds firm, the symbol governs the whole attitude and frees new energy. Founders' trials show traces of this: Jesus and Satan, Buddha and Mara, Luther and the Devil.

Schiller's mediating state repeats an image found in Indian liberation from opposites through yoga, in Brahman, Rita and the Tao. Identical symbols arise without transmission because the unconscious varies less than peoples do; Meister Eckhart resembles the Upanishads with no traceable link. Cosmogonic myth projects how introversion transforms the relation to objects. Indian religion is introverted and gains knowledge; Christian love is extraverted and gains works.

Aestheticism cannot educate humanity, since it presupposes the love of beauty it hopes to create and, being refined hedonism, lacks moral force. Human totality includes the ugly. For Schiller beauty was a religious ideal, and the aesthetic state is really religious devotion: an emptying of consciousness akin to yoga, and a collective phenomenon open to ordinary people. Schiller's "must" is the usual "ought" invoked when no way is visible. Psychology should ask how things are, not whether they are useful or right.

Devotion sinks energy into the unconscious and reactivates the parent complex, from which divine images arise. Reduce the symbol to a sign of the actual parents and it stays regressive; accept it as a real symbol and regression becomes progress. A living symbol draws life toward a distant goal, as Paris and Helen drove Faust onward. Give it conscious value, because the more consciousness cuts itself off from the unconscious, the more dangerous the unconscious's interference becomes.


## Poets and philosophers as witnesses

Schiller's naive and sentimental classify poems, not people, though the mechanisms match: the naive poet is nature and identifies with the object; the sentimental poet seeks nature and confers value by choice. The naive attitude rests on sensation and risks subjection to the object; the sentimental rests on intuition and risks losing reality. Schiller's realist and idealist, however, are true innate types close to extravert and introvert.

Nietzsche saw that Olympian serenity was painted over terror. **Between a people's religion and its actual way of life there is always a compensating relation.** The Christian religion of love presided over enormous butchery. The Apollonian is an inner perception of images, introversion; the Dionysian is an outward flood of archaic feeling-sensation, extraversion. The Dionysian is not aesthetic but raw instinct, and civilized people's dammed instincts are more destructive than those of primitives, who live theirs out in small doses.

Schiller and Nietzsche both turned a religious problem into an aesthetic one, because the aesthetic stance spares the viewer personal involvement. Nietzsche's later turn to Dionysos shows the surrogate failed. Real reconciliation is an unconscious, irrational event that grows like nature, the fruit of yearning and hope. Nietzsche was an intuitive inclined to introversion. His ideas point to two irrational types: intuition, which adapts through faint cues, and sensation, which depends on actual stimuli. Socrates' daemon was intuition so repressed it appeared as hallucination. Jordan's intermediate type resolves into these two.

Each type is shadowed by a primitive opposite. The introvert rises to abstraction partly to master elemental passion; the extravert's adapted feelings rest on crude, egoistic thoughts.

> Hence his golden rule should always be to ask himself: "What is my actual wish and secret purpose?"

A judging observer sees the conscious character, a perceiving observer the unconscious one, so a person can look both introverted and extraverted. Judging someone by their unconscious sees the shadow, and the shadow admits the criticism, to the conscious person's cost. Describe types from conscious psychology, since only the subject knows their motives.

Jordan mixed activity with emotion; activity is better excluded as a criterion. An introverted woman's defining trait is her conscious inner life; her chaotic affect is her unreliable side. Intellectual analysis caricatures the extravert, whose value lies in relations to objects, and only life does them justice. Each type has its own wickedness: the introvert's in coarse deeds, the extravert's in tactless judgments that wound. Introverted feeling is genuine and serves no outside end. Neither type is better, and their tension is needed.

In Spitteler's poem Prometheus serves his soul and Epimetheus the world: two lines of development in one person. The inner world is as real as the outer. The soul relates us to it as the persona relates us to society, and appears distinct and even demonic because the collective unconscious shines through it. Only total surrender to one world reveals this duality. Epimetheus trades his soul for a conventional conscience that brings success and blindness. **Whoever is fixed on outer reality must live their myth unknowingly; whoever turns inward must dream their outer life.** In Goethe's Pandora the roles reverse. A figure granted overwhelming power is a bearer of projected unconscious contents. When the soul couples with the inferior function, the superior one is too collective, and the neglected side turns morbid.

Withdrawn wholly inward, energy must create an equivalent, often a symbol of renewal such as Pandora's jewel. Collective consciousness, judging by existing values, loses it, because only free feeling can value something new. A once-good principle cut off from life turns destructive: blameless Epimetheus ends by handing the divine children to Behemoth. Rejecting a religion intellectually does not free the psyche from it, since religion is an attitude kept alive by inertia. Facing a task beyond its means, energy regresses and revives older forms, as the late eighteenth century revived paganism.

Poets read the collective unconscious and voice what others only dream; anarchism was long foretold by poets. A new living symbol holds evil along with good, or it would lack life and beauty. The deeper a work reaches, the more it is resisted, and deep content can look morbid. Spitteler's poem is less beautiful than Faust or Zarathustra but truer to collective processes. In Faust, Faust and Mephisto are one person, and the magician who keeps a pagan coexistence of opposites can carry the reconciling symbol. The artist is creator and educator whose works trace future development.


## The reconciling symbol across religions

The renewal of God is a universal image for a change of attitude that releases new life. Seasonal parallels are real, but myths use nature as material for psychic processes. The freed energy appears as a young or renewed god. When energy is split between opposed attitudes, life's optimum moves to an irrational middle, projected in the West as a Saviour acting on inscrutable will; the East has long made the middle path reachable by conscious attitude. The divine birth is a constant rhythm of energy going in and out, described mythically because it is unconscious.

Indian thought seeks freedom from the pairs of opposites, heat and cold, love and hate, because suffering is affect and affect follows the opposites. Brahman is their irrational union: being and non-being, still and swift, a state and a creative process. Its symbols, sun, fire, wind and breath, are energy symbols, so Brahman matches the idea of libido. Withdrawing attention from objects lowers consciousness, as hypnosis does, and activates cosmic primordial images. Sacrifice renounces the valuable part, producing a new direction rather than a reversal. Ritual's magic acts on the worshipper, restoring vital energy.

A creative world principle is a projection of living human energy, and energy implies opposition, since current needs two potentials. Manas and Vac, mind and speech, are the inward and outward movements. Personification marks a content as autonomous. When the ego identifies with one function, the rest splits off and returns personified: Faust's poodle becomes Mephistopheles. **Chosen one-sidedness is a mark of high culture; one-sidedness you cannot help is barbarism and turns demonic.**

Rita and the Tao express energy's lawful course. Man stands between Yang and Yin, as Faust felt two souls in one breast, and needs the irrational Tao to avoid being dragged to an extreme. Being irrational, Tao cannot be willed: hence wu-wei, not-doing. The way of energy is vocation and law, not a slide into instinct; primitives produce moralities stricter than ours. Morality is built into life's laws, like a house or a ship.

> There is no morality without freedom. When a barbarian loosens the animal within him, he is not free, but bound.

Westerners still need external law because their barbarism is caged, not conquered. In Parsifal the reconciler is free of both Grail and spear, and the problem is taming libido, not sexuality as such. What matters is the attitude toward sex and power, shaped by collective ideas that gain religious force when they express primordial images. Those images are deposits of adaptive experience evoked by great conflicts; whoever cannot think what they experience simply lives the myth.

Each culture has a reconciling principle: service of God, service of the Self, and in Goethe and Spitteler service of the soul, symbolized by service of woman. Hermas, who wished the beautiful Rhoda were his wife, saw her in a vision as a goddess charging him with sin. Forswearing a desire is not being free of it, and the soul exposes it. By making the beloved divine, the psyche severs the idol from the object and frees the lover from its spell. The beloved's image became the anima, exalted in Dante's Beatrice and the Mother of God. The soul then redirects that energy into the task of the age: Rhoda becomes the Church and a jointless tower. Reason alone balances only those already balanced by reason; most must take on the opposite of their condition. Loss of soul is a complex breaking loose and tyrannizing, as in running amok, and primitive dread of magic protects by placing the source of passion's power in the unconscious.

The Virgin's symbols, tower, rose, enclosed garden, vessel, come from erotic and pagan imagery, translated toward God. The vessel and Grail are non-Christian in origin and show the feminine principle strengthening in medieval men. Folding the individual cult of woman into collective worship of Mary stunted a growing soul-culture; the value owed to real women sank and was projected back as witches. Repressing this tendency also fed heresy, the Inquisition's overcompensated doubt, and the Reformation. Love and individual power exclude each other, a medieval problem Wagner and Nietzsche reopened.

Symbol-formation depreciates the object and so creates freedom; total identity with the object destroys cognition, as when a bushman, furious after a failed day of fishing, wrung the neck of his beloved son. Symbols come from inherited imprints, not personal repression. A symbol lives on held-back energy and directs it, and once it dissolves the energy rushes toward direct discharge. It must be the best available expression and beyond criticism. It dies once its meaning is fully expressed, and none can be manufactured.

For the mystics God is relative to the soul, a return to the primitive sense of God as a force. Sought outside, divine value is projected onto objects, which then dominate; withdrawn into the soul, it brings bliss. The soul both relates us to the unconscious and personifies it. It perceives the unconscious and shapes it into symbols, and the happy state is a creative state. God exists while the soul distinguishes itself from the unconscious force and passes away when it merges back. Projections dissolve only when ripe. Bliss is freely flowing life, like a child's. Life flows from muddy springs as well as clear ones. Image energy goes into art, philosophy, sects or licence. When action is impossible, patient endurance supported by developed fantasy is the right adaptation. Psychology offers only a better metaphor, and mystics signal tides in the collective psyche.

Living religions organize a regression that renews the individual. After the Reformation made God subjective, individualism risked chaos, so the soul must create a new symbol. Reason cannot make it; it comes where least expected, from Nazareth, as a child. It revives the inferior function, turns regression into progress, and ends the one-sided rule of either attitude.

Its arrival brings danger, because it carries what consciousness rejected, and the flood of new energy can reverse all values. People hate it in proportion to their repressed libido. The barbaric part that Christianity never tamed was projected onto those outside it, especially Jews, which fed medieval persecution. That crude divine half was assigned to the devil, so when it rises, God comes as Behemoth. Without real acceptance of the symbol, unconscious impulses slip in disguised as correct ideas.


## Other typologies, read the same way

Pathology magnifies normal traits, so it can inform typology. Gross proposed that how long an idea's after-effect lasts governs later associations. A short after-effect gives the quick, adaptable, shallow, tactless extravert. A long one gives the deep, abstract introvert whose complexes stay isolated and can harden into overvalued ideas. Integrating crude instincts, power as much as sex, is hard for both. Each type mistakes its undifferentiated function for something individual. **Only what is differentiated can be individual; raw passion and raw thought are common to everyone.**

Physiological explanations cannot account for sudden shifts of attitude, since introversion and extraversion are switchable mechanisms. What decides is general psychic tension: tension marks introversion, relaxation extraversion. Ostwald's speed theory fails too. Helmholtz did not react slowly; Helmholtz reacted inwardly, answering students' questions days later with theories they could not connect to their problem.

Worringer's feeling-into is extraversion; abstraction is introversion, a flight from objects felt as threatening. Each rests on an unconscious act: the abstracter's world is already alive through participation mystique, the empathizer's object already emptied. Both are self-divestiture and both are defence and adaptation. Real understanding needs both.

James's tender-minded rationalist and tough-minded empiricist are introvert and extravert, though the labels carry bias. Optimism and pessimism do not belong to either type. Concrete thinking follows objects and sees differences; abstract thinking follows archetypes, which act like invisible stage managers. **Pushed to extremes, both build a mythology, one of cells and atoms, the other of eternal ideas.** They run in mirror image: the introvert shapes material from an unconscious idea and reaches experience, the extravert follows material carrying projected ideas and reaches the idea.

Rationalism is not empiricism's opposite; reason governs both. Real sensationalism's opposite is intuition. Object orientation leans to pluralism, scepticism and determinism; idea orientation to monism, dogmatism, free will and a feeling of power. Absolute devotion to facts is as religious as devotion to God. Whatever is valued most assimilates the person to it. Pragmatism clears prejudice but is a stopgap; resolution needs a creation coordinating opposites, as muscle movement uses antagonists. Nietzsche's Zarathustra is the best example of an intuitive method.

Ostwald's romantic and classic scientists are extravert and introvert. The four temperaments are superficial, judging only outward show. The romantic publishes early, teaches widely and burns out. The classic shows only finished, depersonalized work. Gauss avoided lecturing because unfinished results felt like appearing before strangers in a nightshirt. The introvert seems late to mature, while the seemingly early extravert keeps an infantile inner life.


## The eight types in practice

The extravert's decisions follow objective conditions and prevailing views. **They are adjusted to the moment, not adapted to life's lasting laws, and may go down with an abnormal milieu.** They neglect their own needs, even bodily ones, until symptoms force restraint: a rising singer loses the high notes. Hysteria is their typical neurosis. The unconscious grows egocentric and infantile in proportion to the extraversion. When the conscious attitude is exaggerated, the unconscious turns from compensation to revolt and collapse. A printer who let his business absorb everything poured a revived childhood love of drawing into tasteless products, and the firm failed. Unconscious tendencies become destructive in proportion to how much recognition they are denied. In every type the superior function expresses aim and will; the inferior ones happen to you.

Extraverted thinking takes its standard from facts or received ideas, so it can be abstract if its ideas are borrowed. Too much fact sterilizes it into heaps of data or a poor second-hand idea. Introverted thinking runs from subjective data to subjective ideas. Each sees only the other's faults, and each needs the other's correction.

The extraverted thinker imposes a formula on everyone, most tyrannically on family. Repressed feeling returns as secret selfishness and touchiness, and the formula hardens into dogma; fanaticism is overcompensated doubt. Thinking with priority is synthetic. Thinking subordinate to another function either restates or reduces to "nothing but," like Mephistopheles. Theosophy explains as little as materialism.

> For fanaticism, after all, is merely overcompensated doubt.

Extraverted feeling sincerely conforms to shared values and holds social life together. One such woman said, "But I can't think what I don't feel." Repressed thinking then fastens hideous thoughts on what she loves most.

The extraverted sensation type is the supreme realist, measuring value by the strength of sensation. Exaggerated, they become a crude pleasure-seeker haunted by repressed intuition as phobias and superstition, and that compulsion balances their easygoing laxity. Rapport between rational types rests on shared judgment, between irrational types on shared events; between different types it usually rests on projection. Intuition seeks possibilities and finds every settled situation a prison. The extraverted intuitive seizes a new possibility with enthusiasm and drops it once defined, inspiring others who reap the harvest; repressed sensation returns as hypochondria and unsuitable entanglements.

The introvert is guided by the subjective factor, the psyche's reaction that merges with the object's effect to form a new fact. It is as real as the radius of the earth, because all perception is subjectively conditioned. The age is extraverted and treats "subjective" as a reproach; calling introversion egoism is that prejudice. The introvert's real error is distrusting their own principle. The true subject is the Self, which includes the unconscious and is larger than the ego. Confusing them inflates the ego, and the neglected object returns as a magical power: the more the ego seeks independence, the more objects enslave it. Psychoasthenia, sensitivity with exhaustion, is the typical neurosis.

Introverted thinking prizes new views over new facts and draws conviction from the archetype. It stays valuable only while connected to known facts. Both extremes are sterile: "it is, therefore it is" and "I think, therefore I think." The type seems cold and stubborn yet is easily exploited, and when it identifies its truth with its person it turns bitter. Its archaic unconscious holds the coming possibility.

Introverted feeling is intense, not wide, and shows mostly as reserve; still waters run deep. It needs art to communicate. When the ego is confused with the Self, it sours into petty tyranny and imagined plots.

Introverted sensation perceives the archetypal background of things, so several painters of one landscape produce different pictures. The type is unpredictable, calm outside, living unknowingly in a mythic world, while repressed intuition scents dark possibilities.

Introverted intuition sees inner images as clearly as sensation sees things. In giddiness, sensation notes the vertigo; intuition sees a tottering man pierced by an arrow. It can foresee events through the archetypes. The type is a seer or an artist-crank, and without them there would have been no prophets in Israel. Its moral variety becomes a voice crying in the wilderness. Its unconscious is a primitive sensation type. Introverted irrational types are underrated but must still express themselves.

Real people show a dominant function and an auxiliary of the other kind: thinking pairs with sensation or intuition, never feeling. Equal consciousness of functions marks a primitive mind. Approach the inferior function through the auxiliary; forcing it directly creates dependence on the physician.


## Clear terms, and why theories differ

Most of the psyche cannot be measured, so precise concepts must stand in for measurement. Abstraction withdraws interest to isolate the essential, and it works in every function: abstract feeling, aesthetic sensation, symbolic intuition. Affect is feeling with bodily disturbance, not at your disposal, whereas feeling can be.

Libido is psychic energy understood as intensity or value. Instinct is any process whose energy consciousness cannot steer; will is the energy it can. Once-conscious habits are automatic, not instinctive. Collective contents include feelings and functions. Identity is an unconscious sameness with the object, behind participation mystique and the urge to correct in others what needs changing in yourself. Introjection draws the object in; projection moves a subjective content into the object and gets its name once identity becomes disturbing.

Fantasy is energy appearing as images. Active fantasy, where consciousness shapes unconscious hints, is the highest psychic activity; passive fantasy signals dissociation and needs criticism. Dream figures can be read on the objective plane, as real people, or on the subjective plane, as parts of yourself, since the imago is never the object.

The persona is a collective mask. The soul is the inner attitude to the unconscious, complementing it with what it lacks: the harsh tyrant has bad dreams. It is contrasexual, anima in men and animus in women. Identifying with the persona projects the soul onto someone, creating a magical tie of love, hate or fear. If the soul is not projected, the person identifies with it and adapts poorly outward. The distinction parallels Nakae Toju's true and false self.

> But the symbol always presupposes that the chosen expression is the best possible description, or formula, of a relatively unknown fact; a fact, however, which is none the less recognized or postulated as existing.

A sign stands for something known. Whether something is a symbol depends largely on the observer, and judgment must decide symptom from symbol. A living social symbol unites the most developed expression with something primitive enough to be shared by all. Only a mind holding its highest and lowest sides in equal conscious tension can create a new one.

Consciousness is the relation of contents to the ego and is not the whole psyche. The unconscious is a boundary concept justified by experience, such as hysterical amnesia reversed by hypnosis. It holds forgotten, repressed and subliminal material, plus mythological images never conscious. Speculating beyond that is idle.

Every philosophy rests on a typical premise, which is why it finds followers. **Recognizing types could move disputes onto psychological ground, as a court stands above contending parties.** Other classifications are possible. In psychology the process is both object and subject, so a theorist's psychology is their own, and admitting types costs a theory most of its claim. One process can be explained reductively, from shared origins, and constructively, toward differentiation, and both are needed. Intellect yields at best half the truth. Every theory of the psyche is itself a psychic process expressing a type, and only the cooperation of such views can reach a higher synthesis.
