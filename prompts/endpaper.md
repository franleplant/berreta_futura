# Endpaper process

The endpaper is the inside face of the printed cover sheet. The A4 cover
sheet is printed on both sides and folded once: the outside carries the
back and front covers, and the inside carries one landscape A4 pattern
that the reader sees as the inside front and inside back covers, running
across the fold. The cover is already picked; the endpaper answers it.

Every round proposes EIGHT endpaper briefs, each generated once. The
editor picks one.

## The job: a pattern that belongs to this cover

An endpaper is a small drawing, or a small family of drawings, repeated
edge to edge like a printed storybook endpaper or a gift wrap. It is the
cover's echo, not a second cover: the reader should open the issue,
see the pattern, and recognise the front cover's object, joke, or world
in it.

- Take the motifs from the cover's world, never its composition. Split
  the cover into its elements (an animal, an object, a prop) and repeat
  those on their own, mixed with two or three related props of the same
  world. Never repeat the cover's scene itself (one thing inside or on
  top of another, exactly as the cover stages it).
- Start the palette from the picked cover's inks and widen it: add two
  or three cheerful accent colours that sit well with them (a sunny
  yellow, a leaf green, a tangerine, a teal), named concretely, so the
  motifs come in four or five colours and the sheet never reads as
  monochrome. The ground is a warm off-white or a very pale tint, never
  a saturated full-page fill and never bare white.
- Medium density: small motifs, dozens of repeats, comfortable breathing
  room between them, but no large empty areas. Lively, playful drawing;
  neither crammed nor sparse.

Make the eight briefs eight different pattern structures, so the editor
chooses between real alternatives. Draw from these, one each:

1. tossed: motifs scattered in varied poses and rotations over a ground
2. rows or half-drop: one motif in tidy rows, each repeat a small variation
3. lattice or knot: the motif's own shape interlocking into a continuous net
4. damask or medallion: an ornamental wallpaper repeat built from the motif
5. stamp grid: a grid of tiles, each tile a different tiny vignette
6. trails: motifs travelling along looping dotted or drawn paths
7. stripes: a block-printed folk band repeat with the motif between borders
8. all-over: many small related objects spread evenly, close but never crammed

## Fixed rules

- No text, letters, numbers, logos, or masthead anywhere in the pattern.
- No border, frame, vignette, centred focal point, or single large figure.
- Never the recurring cast. The endpaper is decorative; characters live
  inside the issue.
- Medium: the art direction's print look (risograph grain, slight
  misregistration) on visible paper, drawn by hand, never clean vector.

The pipeline appends the sheet format (landscape A4, full bleed, the fold)
and the reference-image note to every prompt, so a prompt describes only
the pattern: motif, structure, density, palette, and medium.
