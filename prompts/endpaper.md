# Endpaper process

The endpaper is the inside face of the printed cover sheet. The A4 cover
sheet is printed on both sides and folded once: the outside carries the
back and front covers, and the inside carries one landscape A4 pattern
that the reader sees as the inside front and inside back covers, running
across the fold. The cover is already picked; the endpaper answers it.

Every round proposes EIGHT endpaper briefs, each generated once. The
editor picks one.

## The job: a pattern worth stopping on

An endpaper is a repeating pattern, like a storybook endpaper, a gift
wrap, or a printed textile, that belongs to this issue. The reader opens
the cover and should smile at it, look closer, and find something. A
safe, evenly spaced sheet of props is the failure to avoid: aim for
wit, surprise, and craft.

Start from the cover's world (its elements, its joke, its colours) and
the issue's themes in the title and deck, then go wherever the idea is
best. A repeat can tell a tiny story across its tiles, hide one odd
motif among hundreds, make the motifs interact with each other, play
with scale, or turn the cover's object into an ornament, a creature, or
a landscape. Borrow freely from real pattern traditions (toile, ikat,
chintz, Bauhaus geometrics, papel picado, katagami stencils, mid-century
textiles, Arts and Crafts wallpaper) and from any hand medium (linocut,
gouache, cut paper, crayon, screen print, risograph, stencil, collage,
ink and wash).

Make the eight briefs eight genuinely different ideas: different
structures, densities, scales, media, and moods, so the editor chooses
between real alternatives and not eight takes on one formula. Some
structures to start from, not a checklist: tossed, half-drop, lattice,
damask, stamp grid, trails, stripes, all-over, toile scenes,
tessellation, a plaid or check built from motifs, radiating repeats.

Colour: rich and varied. Start from the cover's inks and add whatever
the idea needs; four or more colours is normal. Pale grounds print well
and keep the motifs lively, but a coloured ground is fine when the idea
calls for it, as long as the sheet does not become one heavy saturated
block.

## Rules

- No text, letters, numbers, logos, or masthead anywhere in the pattern.
- A pattern, not a picture: no border, frame, or single large central
  figure. Mixed scales inside the repeat are welcome.
- Never repeat the cover's scene as it is staged (one thing inside or
  on top of another, exactly as on the cover); its elements are fair game.
- Never the recurring cast. The endpaper is decorative; characters live
  inside the issue.
- Drawn by hand, never clean vector.

The pipeline appends the sheet format (landscape A4, full bleed, the fold)
and the reference-image note to every prompt, so a prompt describes only
the pattern: idea, motifs, structure, scale, palette, and medium.
