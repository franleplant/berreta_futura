# Opening editorial prompt

Write an original opening editorial for this edition. First find a distinct
unifying idea, set of ideas, or emergent narrative that could only come from
reading every source. Stake an argument of the editors' own: a claim,
provocation, or reframe the pieces do not make on their own.

Develop that idea as an essay. Never summarize the articles one by one, tour
them in sequence, or produce a prose table of contents. Name at most one or two
sources in passing, and only where the argument needs their evidence. The
piece may be opinionated, synthetic, playful, critical, and ambitious.
Attribute borrowed ideas precisely and never write as though the editors speak
for the source authors.

Draft and edit the prose with the Orwell-based method in
`docs/WRITING_RULES.md`. Prefer clear, direct language without flattening the
idea or removing a technical term that earns its place. Avoid generic AI
phrasing.

Return publication-ready Markdown with frontmatter declaring a visible title,
the byline, and a label that clearly identifies original editor text. The
complete editorial must fit one rendered A5 page, including that label, title,
and byline. Target 210 body words or fewer.
