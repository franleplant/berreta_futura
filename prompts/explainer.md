The reader is a working programmer who has never looked inside this system. Teach it in three passes, each standing on the one before:

As a user: what it is, the few things you actually touch, and what you can count on.
As a developer: what each call does, the limits and guarantees you design around, and the mistakes that cost you.
Inside: follow one request through the system, part by part, then the mechanisms that make it fast, durable, and correct.

Define every term in plain words the first time it appears. Include a very simple unifying example, plainly hypothetical and technically correct, and carry it through all three passes.

Write in your own voice and name the source behind a claim when it matters who said it; never refer to “the article”. Never make the sources say anything they did not say: keep their claims at their strength, their hedges, their tenses, their numbers. Your own sentences, the opening version and the example, obey the same rule. Where the sources leave a gap, leave it rather than fill it from memory.

Start with a 30 second version, with no heading or label on it. Do not use numbered titles. Don’t repeat. Land at most one aphorism per piece; end sections plainly. Do not use paraprosdokians ever.

Use correct capitalization, punctuation, and spacing.

Use as few words as understanding allows.
