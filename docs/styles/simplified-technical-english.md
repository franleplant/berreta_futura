# Style overlay: Simplified Technical English

This overlay sits on top of `docs/WRITING_STYLE.md`. Where the two conflict
inside explanatory prose, this overlay wins; everywhere else the blended voice
governs unchanged.

Write the explanatory body in the spirit of Simplified Technical English
(ASD-STE100):

- Short sentences. Aim for twenty words or fewer.
- One instruction or one idea per sentence. Never two joined by "and" or "which"
  to save a sentence break.
- Active voice throughout: an actor does something to an object.
- One name per concept, used the same way every time it appears. Do not vary the
  term for elegance; a reader learning the concept needs to recognize the same
  word twice, not admire a synonym.
- Simple, consistent verb tenses. Do not shift tense mid-explanation.
- No idioms and no figurative language in a procedural or definitional passage:
  a mechanism's moving parts, a walkthrough's steps, a term's definition. Say
  the thing plainly.
- Prefer the shortest form that stays accurate. Approximate nothing to be brief.

What this overlay does **not** change:

- The opening and the closing, which follow the blended voice.
- Narrative asides, scene-setting, and the piece's own judgments.
- Every fidelity rule. Simplified Technical English is a constraint on how a
  sentence is built, never a licence to simplify a claim, drop a qualification,
  or state something the source does not support. When plain phrasing would
  make a claim stronger than the evidence, keep the qualification and spend the
  words.
- Orwell's rule vi still outranks it: a sentence no person would say is wrong
  however short and however active.
