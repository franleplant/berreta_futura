# Style overlay: narrative first

This overlay sits on top of `docs/WRITING_STYLE.md` and changes how you decide
the shape of the piece. Everything about voice, fidelity and length stands.

Before you plan the article, find the story the source is telling.

Most sources contain one, whether or not the author noticed. Something was
believed, something happened, and now something is different. A team shipped an
agent that wrote beautifully and lied a third of the time, measured it, and
found the missing layer. A protocol dropped the state it had been carrying and
the shape of every client changed. That movement — a situation, a turn, a
consequence — is what a reader retains a week later, and it is what a list of
accurate points never gives them.

Write the piece as that movement.

- Name the story in one sentence before you draft: **who or what was in a
  situation, what turned, what is different now.** If you cannot write that
  sentence from the source, this overlay does not apply to the piece; write it
  straight and well instead. Do not manufacture a turn.
- Open inside the situation, at a concrete moment. Not at the topic, not at
  background, not at a definition.
- Let the reader feel the turn as a turn. Something stops working, someone
  measures the thing nobody measured, a decision arrives with a cost.
- Land on what is different now, for someone who does the work.
- Everything that does not move the story is a candidate for cutting. Explanation
  is not exempt: teach the mechanism at the point in the story where the reader
  needs it to understand what just happened, and not before.

## What this is not

Not fiction, and not drama laid over reporting. You may not invent a scene, a
character, a motive, a sequence of events, or a moment of realisation the source
does not support. You may not turn a hedge into a discovery, or a technical
choice into a conflict, because a story wants one. Every element of the
narrative must be something the source actually says happened.

Where the source is genuinely not a story — a specification, a reference, a set
of parallel observations — say so in your working notes and write it plainly.
A forced narrative is worse than a clear list, and `craft` will read the strain
as false profundity.

The test: a reader should be able to tell someone else what happened, in three
sentences, a week later, and be right.
