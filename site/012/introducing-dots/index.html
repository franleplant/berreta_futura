Introducing dots

By OpenAI

From OpenAI’s news page.

Read the original openai.com

Dots are always-on agents powered by GPT-6 Astra. Each has its own cloud computer, learns from feedback over time, and can work toward your goals 24/7, connecting to over 4,000 apps through our plugins. You reach yours in ChatGPT, Slack, or Teams, or on a voice call. We’re beginning to roll them out across Pro, Business Premium, and Enterprise plans in eligible markets. Dots can still make mistakes, so always review consequential work.

What a dot does

A dot can do nearly anything with its own computer, its own browser, and the apps you’ve connected. You can open its computer at any time to inspect its work, and you can give it permission to use your laptop. It can run with a project while working on several others, and you can keep bringing it new tasks without juggling separate threads or directing every step.

The more you work together, the more it learns your preferences, how you think, and what good looks like to you. Sometimes it brings you work before you think to ask. Inside OpenAI, a bug appears in Slack and dots immediately start investigating; a new design arrives and dots turn it into a working app. Outside OpenAI, an early tester’s dot noticed he’d forgotten to invoice a publication, prepared the invoice, and sent it after his approval.

For a developer, a dot watches customer feedback for recurring requests, builds and tests the smaller fixes, and brings complete PRs to review with videos of the changes. For a scientist, it reruns analyses as new data arrives, investigates unexpected results, updates the figures, and flags what needs review.

Dots carry context across every channel, so yours has the full picture wherever you reach it.

You’re in control

Each dot works on its own cloud computer; yours stays separate unless you choose to connect it. For supported websites, dots can use saved passwords without exposing them to the model. When you aren’t working with it, your dot does what we call “proactive research” in the background, using read-only tools that can’t send messages, change app content, or control your browser or computer. If our monitoring detects a safety concern, it can pause or stop the dot’s work.

You choose which apps dots can access. They start with built-in rules for when to act and when to ask; Custom Rules let you allow specific actions, require approval, or block them. Auto-review checks actions that could affect your accounts or share information. Certain sensitive tasks, such as changing a password, always stay with you. You can follow progress, including background work, in Activity View.

We don’t use content from Business, Enterprise, or Edu workspaces to improve our models by default; on personal plans, you control it. We don’t train directly on proactive research or your dot’s notes to itself, though information from them may be used if it helps inform an eligible conversation or task, depending on your settings.

Specialist dots

Specialist dots take on dedicated responsibilities inside an organization, each with its own identity, credentials, and access. We’re starting with focused enterprise pilots, building on early testing inside OpenAI across procurement, invoice processing, email marketing, customer support, and commercial contracting. We’re also working with Microsoft to integrate them with Agent 365.

Getting started

Your first dot is included in Pro or Business Premium at no extra cost, with an allowance for deeper work and extended limits for the first month. Enterprise users, including Edu and Healthcare, can try the beta when their workspace admin enables it. Conversations with your dot don’t count toward your ChatGPT usage limits; tasks it starts in Codex or ChatGPT Work do. In the future, you’ll be able to add more dots and scale each one’s speed or monthly workload.

Create your first dot in the ChatGPT desktop app or a desktop browser, connect your apps, and let it introduce itself. After setup, you can message it from mobile.
