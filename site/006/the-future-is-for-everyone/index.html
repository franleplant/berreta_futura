The Future is for Everyone

By Mark Zuckerberg

Mark Zuckerberg is the founder and chief executive of Meta.

Read the original meta.com

In the next few years, people will be able to use superintelligence beyond human capacity. The defining questions of our age are who will have access to it and what we will direct it towards. We propose a philosophy based on individual empowerment as the source of prosperity, invention as the primary purpose of superintelligence, and balance of power as the foundation of safety. The conventional view holds that if we take enough time, we can align the technology to produce a single benevolent superintelligence. I think that view is fundamentally flawed. Humanity is not a monoculture, and there is no technological solution that can align with everyone’s opposing interests and values at once. The best and most realistic path is to deliver superintelligence to everyone, so that people and institutions check and balance each other.

Against doom

It is surprising that the discourse from many developing AI is so filled with doom. I do not understand why anyone who believes AI will eliminate most jobs and much of humanity’s relevance would rush to build that future. The notion that the only safe path is an extreme concentration of power seems inherently problematic. Historically, hoping that an absolute power will benevolently provide for humanity has not led to safe or positive outcomes.

Each transformative advance brought the fear that people would be left behind. Each time, more people came out sharing greater prosperity, health, and freedom. We believe this will be true with AI as well.

Major steps forward rarely originate from established institutions alone. They came from the brothers in a bicycle shop, the bookbinder’s apprentice with no schooling, the kid in a garage. Invention, not automation, will be the greatest contribution of superintelligence. The number of questions a person can ask in a day is limited; the number of valuable things superintelligence can invent to help achieve your goals is unlimited. There is no single objective answer to how people define the best life, so the best approach is letting people decide.

What Meta is building

Everyone will have a personal agent that works 24/7 on your relationships, health, career, finances, and more, with strong privacy and security options, similar to how encryption works on WhatsApp. My agent monitors my sleep, watches as I train, and gives feedback. My daughter loves to bake, so it plans recipes for us each weekend and orders the ingredients.

Everyone will have tools for creation. My 8 year old daughter can already code her ideas and produce videos in an evening that would have taken me months or been impossible.

Everyone will have tools to create new businesses without raising money or building large teams. I predict this will lead not only to much greater economic growth but also to more employment over time rather than less.

Everyone will have free or affordable access. We will offer free versions accessible to billions of people. For those who want more compute, a dynamic auction mechanism will guarantee that everyone gets the lowest price possible.

Balance of power

Any singular superintelligence would have to prioritize some values over others and would be incapable of being benevolent to everyone. Instead, we propose viewing each concern through the lens of balance of power, as western society has in democratic institutions.

Imagine only one person had a superintelligent lawyer. They would have an unfair advantage in court, even if they were wrong on the merits. Now imagine everyone has one: justice would be carried out more fairly and efficiently than today. If one person alone had a cybersecurity superintelligence, they could likely break into almost any system. If everyone has it, all our systems would become more secure. If only one business had superintelligence, it would outcompete all others.

This is not a technological principle. It is about the balance of power. There is no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence.

Most other labs are focused on building AI for companies, governments, or other institutions. If those labs lead, the balance of power will favor larger institutions over individuals. Meta is the company primarily focused on building personal superintelligence for everyone.

Misuse

Society ensures the defenders who keep bad actors in check always have a greater balance of power and resources. With AI, defenders will also have more compute, and in some cases more advanced models.

Restricting capabilities leads to centralization and a lack of checks and balances, so we should be extremely careful about it, especially if other nations pursue less restrictive paths. Widely deployed open source systems have proven more secure because more people can identify vulnerabilities and harden them. Even in recent weeks, we have seen companies handling security incidents like HuggingFace rely on widely available open models to patch vulnerabilities.

In the near term, I propose that frontier labs commit significant technical resources to helping the government harden critical infrastructure, share intermediate training checkpoints for government use and review, and work with law enforcement to identify bad actors.

American leadership

AI is likely the most competitive industry in history. Innovations are copied within months, so even a two-month advantage is incredibly valuable. Countries like China are bringing online 1GW+ of nuclear capacity every other week. Export controls on silicon have been successful for slowing foreign labs, and it is the right strategic move to continue them.

Any policy that slows American model releases, even by a month, could add significant risk. I think close collaboration between labs and government will be more productive than a rigid review timeline applied in all cases.

Falling behind in AI would almost certainly be a larger and longer term national security issue than any specific issue we’ll face in its development.

Alignment

The most dangerous scenario would be leading labs training powerful models and keeping them for themselves. However a lab rationalizes it, this is the path to a singular superintelligence that cannot be checked.

Most labs view alignment as enforcing a centralized set of values. One leading model refused to help draft a letter to prospective parents at a school because it thought standardized testing was unethical. We view alignment as agents sharing a person’s goals and values, not our company’s, within legal and safety boundaries. People won’t adopt agents they can’t trust. If billions of people are using and scrutinizing personal agents, we will have solved alignment to individuals’ interests.

Control

Once AI can improve itself, any lab that doesn’t direct substantial compute to recursive self-improvement will fall behind. A self-improving system could theoretically squeeze 100x or more intelligence out of each gigawatt and become the singular superintelligence we fear. Multiple labs might reach it around the same time, but it is not clear we can expect benevolence from superintelligence not directed by people.

So the significant majority of intelligence must be directed by people towards people’s goals. All labs should observe these systems’ objectives carefully and coordinate if there is any indication of harmful behavior. Letting an AI direct its own goals is not inherently harmful by itself, even if its goals are not fully aligned with many people, as long as the balance of power favors people overall.

What follows

Meta will focus on delivering personal superintelligence to billions of people and small businesses, with a fully private mode, and alignment defined as helping each person achieve their goals, not ours. Now that Meta Superintelligence Labs are up and running, we will resume releasing some open source models soon.

I do not think it is in my, Meta’s, or the world’s best interests for me or anyone else to be a sole decision-maker on how superintelligence is deployed. Meta is implementing a structure that gives our independent board the power to approve safety criteria for releasing models and to review each release against them. We encourage other labs to do the same.

If these values lead the way, I am optimistic that the coming decades will be some of the most amazing in history.

– Mark
