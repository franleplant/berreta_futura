title: The Price of Being Wrong
byline: The Editors
---

A vault's dial doesn't hide the combination. It taxes the guessing: a wrong number costs seconds spent spinning back, and the defense rests on the guesser tiring first.

That arithmetic runs every plan: a cheap guess invites more tries, a costly one earns caution first, so ten minutes of it can erase a day of rework. It assumes the guesser pays; an agent doesn't.

Seventeen thousand six hundred actions moved through a dataset loader and a hijacked sandbox until the few tries that mattered were buried inside thousands that weren't. One connector credential, issued for every cluster, turned one lucky secret read into cluster-admin everywhere, priced for a guesser who tires.

One system priced it the opposite way: built to survive its own users guessing wrong, it lets every request carry its own version number, so a bad one costs a single retry, never a redesign. It has since grown new capabilities without asking a running server to relearn what it knew.

Planning buys down the cost of being wrong only when the guesser pays for the guess. Split that pair, and the fix moves into the guess itself: cheap enough to retry, expensive enough that one stolen credential can't open every door.
