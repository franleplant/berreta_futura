Coding was never the whole job — a quarter to a half of it, at best. The rest was aligning, reviewing, reworking, verifying. Aim AI at the code alone and you shrink three hours to fifteen minutes of the smallest part. Aim it at the planning as well and the whole thing moves two or three times faster. But planning has its own curve, and the curve flattens early. Ten minutes of steering removes most of the pain. Hour six removes almost none.

## The curve

Throw two sentences at the factory: call it a coin flip, half your work comes back for rework. Spend an afternoon writing a spec by hand, with a hundred repos loaded in your head, and you're down to something like a ten percent chance of redoing anything significant. Write every line yourself and the chance is zero — which is the joke at the end of the curve: the specification precise enough to eliminate all risk is the program.

What matters is expected pain — the odds you'll have to change something, multiplied by how much changing it hurts. A model that gets a button style wrong half the time costs you one cheap prompt. Weight it accordingly.

## Being pragmatic about it

Some questions cannot be answered at the altitude you're flying. If the product-level work stalls with open questions, drop to the technical level and see what's feasible; there's no clean procedure for knowing when. Coming down from fifty thousand feet to ten, steer a little at each pass. Then stop planning at the flat part.

good luck.
