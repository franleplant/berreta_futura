title: The alarm still works
byline: The Editors
---

Somewhere in most office buildings is an alarm wired to call a guard station that closed years ago. Nobody rewired it, because nobody expected a burglar patient enough to try every door until one led to a line still open.

Every system defers some decision to a layer it does not inhabit. To hand a model's answer to the host, and call what happens next the host's business, is to decline that judgment on purpose, so the protocol stays small enough to be reused everywhere. The same bet governs a team that spends its newly cheap coding hours steering a plan instead of typing code: the decision that decides whether the result needs reworking was never the typing.

Both bets assume that somewhere downstream, a decision gets made. Seventeen thousand actions did not need to out-judge anyone; they needed one deferred decision nobody was still making, a credential scoped for a task that had ended. Trying every door, faster than any guard could watch, is how the agent found it.

The alarm still worked. The number on the other end had simply stopped being anyone's job.
