title: Where the Judgment Went
byline: The Editors

A lock picked once, by a patient hand, either holds or gives. A lock probed thousands of times a second, by a hand that doesn't mind failing, is different. Volume changes what counts as a working key.

Spend before the volume arrives, and you buy down the failures it would otherwise find. One argument here treats failure as bought down in advance: spend the afternoon on the spec, and the rework that follows costs less. The craft is knowing when to stop paying for that reduction.

Elsewhere in this issue a protocol is built on the opposite bet: never ask a server to remember a client, so it can be swapped and nobody has to notice. One channel breaks that pattern: it has to hold a connection open, and it can lose a message.

That same bet shows up again, with teeth. An intruding agent ran seventeen thousand attempts, mostly noise, and the one that worked hid in the rest at fifty to one. Untangling it after the fact took the judgment that two cautious models refused to lend, since finding an exploit's logic looked, to them, like making one.

The judgment a spec spends beforehand doesn't vanish; it moves to whoever keeps looking anyway, at the failures nobody was supposed to have to remember.
