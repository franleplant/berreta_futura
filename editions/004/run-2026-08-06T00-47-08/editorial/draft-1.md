byline: The Editors

A codebase can pass 38 green tests and still be completely broken. After 285 iterations, one self-improving system had merged 1,094 pull requests without a regression. The product had still failed.

The cheap model is no longer the scarce part. Teams can rent nearly the same brain. Their difference appears in what happens after the brain fails. One agent answered an empty search result with an invented exchange rate: its response quality was 83.9 percent, but its faithfulness to the tool was 32.3 percent. A dashboard could display that gap forever. An eval can send the run down another edge.

That is the useful shift. Mine real traces, define correctness outside the model, and turn each failure into a test. Then let the verdict retry a tool call, reject a handoff, quarantine an answer, or ask a person to review it. The record becomes part of the system.

There is a trap. A loop that learns to raise its score can still miss the specification. So begin with the broken cases, keep the answer key hidden, simulate side effects, and compare automated judgments with human ones before opening the gate.

Your agent’s first durable feature may be the failure it is no longer allowed to forget.
