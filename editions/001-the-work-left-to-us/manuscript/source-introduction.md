This issue begins with a post by Arvind Narayanan pointing readers toward the annotated version of his ICML 2026 invited keynote, *What will be left for us to work on?*, delivered in Seoul on July 9, 2026.

The post is the trailhead; the annotated slides and edited transcript are the substantive work. Narayanan develops three connected arguments: AI is best understood through the processes by which powerful technologies become products and diffuse through society; recursive self-improvement does not automatically collapse several distinct ideas about advanced AI into a single sudden economic event; and human effort may move increasingly from execution toward evaluation, judgment, direction, and control.

The talk closes with a proposal for human-AI co-superintelligence: people using increasingly capable systems to raise the ceiling of what they can understand and accomplish, rather than autonomous systems becoming humanity's replacement.
