title: Capability is the beginning, not the destination
---

The talk asks readers to stop drawing a straight line from a laboratory result to a social outcome. Between them is a chain of work:

1. **Capability.** A system can perform a task under some conditions.
2. **Product.** The capability is made reliable, usable, affordable, and supportable.
3. **Adoption.** Organizations change workflows, responsibilities, data, incentives, and controls.
4. **Adaptation.** Professions, institutions, regulators, and communities respond over time.
5. **Consequence.** Benefits, harms, power, and new forms of work are distributed through society.

The chain is not an excuse for complacency. It is a map of where intervention remains possible. Every arrow contains design decisions, institutional bargaining, and political choices.

The second movement separates questions that are often bundled together: whether recursive self-improvement is technically possible; whether it produces a sudden capability discontinuity; whether such capability diffuses quickly into economic power; and whether autonomous systems are granted real-world authority. Treating these as one event makes a dramatic story. Treating them separately makes a useful analysis.

The final movement changes the question from "What tasks survive?" to "Where does responsibility move?" As execution becomes cheaper, effort can shift toward deciding, evaluating, steering, and preserving human understanding. Those are not consolation prizes. They determine what the machinery is for.
