Every month I spend more money on inference, and so does everyone on my team and everyone I know in AI. We are not paying for more software; we are paying for tokens. Cheaper models and subscriptions trim the bill, yet the number of tokens generated keeps growing, and it's growing exponentially. Agents are becoming more powerful every month, people run more of them, and money that once paid lawyers, accountants, translators, and developers now goes to inference. Inference will eat the software world.

## The bill keeps growing

There are optimizations. Open-source models from China often greatly over-perform closed models from America, giving far more value for far less cost. Subscriptions like OpenCode Go give access to near-frontier models for 20x less than a Claude Max subscription.

Despite all of them, the tokens burned rise month after month. OpenRouter data, Vercel AI gateway data, estimates from OpenAI's and Anthropic's API: wherever you look, the number keeps growing. Not slowly, not linearly, but exponentially.

## Why agents will do most of the work

Models are improving every one to two months, with no sign of slowing; if anything, they are speeding up. Harnesses are improving as fast. A year ago we had basically only Claude Code; now we have Pi, Codex, Hermes, OMP, Droid, Prime Agent, and many others.

Both point to one conclusion: every month, agents can do a larger percentage of all software tasks. That leads to a world where agents do the vast majority of interactions with all software that exists.

People are choosing banks based on which one has an API. People are ordering food through the DoorDash CLI, because they can tell a personal agent what they want instead of using a bad UI. The more APIs, the better Computer Use, the more connectors, the more agents will be able to do.

Each person, at least those who are locked-in, now runs several agents in parallel. Are you running more agents than you were six or twelve months ago? I don't have to know you to know the answer is yes.

More agents means more tokens, and more tokens means more money spent on inference.

## Where the money comes from

A simple legal question used to mean paying a human lawyer; now you ask ChatGPT Pro. Categorizing thousands of accounting transactions used to mean paying an accountant; now you tell Claude Code to write a Python script. You don't pay translators; you ask a chatbot. You don't pay someone to research; you ask Perplexity. You don't pay a frontend developer; you have Fable do it.

Money that would have gone to other computer work, products or services, now goes to inference.

## What is left

After wrestling with this idea for many days, I found some industries, fields, and tasks that agents, no matter how smart, will not be able to do. That is a topic for another article.

For now: anything easily verifiable, anything with a clear objective function, will be done sooner or later by AI agents, whether you like it or not. Inference will be one of the biggest markets in the world, and the biggest in all of computer work. What you do with this information is up to you.
