Every month I spend more on inference. So does my team, so does everyone I know in this space, and it's probably true for you too. We're not paying for more vibe-coded AI SaaS. We're paying for tokens.

Optimizations exist. Open-source models out of China often greatly over-perform the closed American ones, and a subscription like OpenCode Go buys near-frontier models for twenty times less than Claude Max. Costs per token fall. Tokens burned still rise, month after month, and not linearly. OpenRouter, the Vercel AI gateway, estimates off the OpenAI and Anthropic APIs: everywhere you look, the curve is exponential.

From first principles the reason is plain. Models improve every one to two months with no sign of slowing, and if anything they're speeding up. A year ago there was basically only Claude Code; now there's Pi, Codex, Hermes, OMP, Droid, Prime Agent, and others. Agents are getting more powerful, which means each month they can do a larger share of all the software tasks there are. People are choosing banks by which one has an API. People are ordering food through the DoorDash CLI, because talking to your own agent beats another shitty UI. More APIs, better Computer Use, more connectors, more the agents can do.

And you're running more of them in parallel than you were six or twelve months ago. I don't need to know you to know the answer. More agents means more tokens, and more tokens means more money on inference.

Watch where the money moves. You had a simple legal question and paid a lawyer; now you ask ChatGPT Pro. You had thousands of transactions to categorize and paid an accountant; now you tell Claude Code to write you a Python script. You don't pay a translator, you don't pay a researcher, you don't pay a frontend developer to polish the site. Every one of those payments still leaves your account. It arrives somewhere else.

After wrestling with this for days I did find things that can't be replaced by throwing more tokens at them, fields agents won't touch however smart they get. That's another article. For now: anything easily verifiable, anything with a clear objective function, gets done by agents sooner or later, whether you like it or not.

Inference will be the biggest market in all of computer-work. What you do with that is up to you.
