title: The Pen Moves Faster Than Review
byline: The Editors
---

The rules you write down for a machine begin to rot the day you write them, and keeping them current is the actual job.

In 1980 Digital Equipment Corporation put a rule-based program called XCON into production to configure VAX orders before they shipped. It saved the company real money for most of a decade. It grew to thousands of rules, and the products it described kept changing, so DEC paid a standing staff of knowledge engineers to keep the rules honest. The knowledge was the asset, and the asset spoiled.

What is new is who holds the pen. A scheduled job can now read how people responded to its last run and open a pull request against the file that instructs it. A human merges. For the first time the upkeep scales with the knowledge instead of against it.

The reviewer is now the narrow part. Rules will arrive faster than anyone reads them. Check this in a year: open your agent's instruction files, pick ten rules, and ask who wrote each one, when, and whether it is still true. If you cannot answer for most of them, you have rebuilt XCON with a faster typist, and the person who pays is whoever inherits it.
