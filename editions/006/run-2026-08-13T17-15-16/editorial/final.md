title: When Keys Matter More Than Price
byline: The Editors

402 has sat in the HTTP spec since 1999, reserved for future use. Now it carries a payload, and the payload names a price. The pitch is that the payment is the credential: no account, no API key, nobody's customer.

Then read the deferred scheme. The client signs the request. Its public key lives in a hosted JWK directory. The server validates the signature and attributes the payment to the associated account. That is an account. That is an API key with better math.

That is also the part that will matter. A tenth of a cent per tide table is a demo. The money is the crawler that reads the whole archive and pays once at midnight, and nobody extends credit to a stranger. Credit needs a name.

So the tide-table publisher gets a customer list again and does not own it. The directory that vouches for keys becomes the gate, and gates get sold. Within two years the fight over paid crawling will not be about price. It will be about whose keys are listed.

---

167 words. The claim the article resists: its own headline property, "no account," survives only in the per-request demo and dies in the deferred path it proposes.
