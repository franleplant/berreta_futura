The question is not whether superintelligence arrives but who holds it, and I believe it should be everyone. Concentrating it in a few institutions is not the safe path but the dangerous one, because no single system can be benevolent to people whose values conflict. Prosperity comes from individual empowerment, the purpose of the technology is invention rather than automation, and safety comes from a balance of power. Meta will build personal superintelligence, offer it free or as cheaply as we can, keep a fully private mode, align it to your goals rather than ours, and resume releasing some open source models soon.

## What it is for

Early AI answered questions. Soon it will help discover new knowledge, from drugs that cure a disease in your family to better ways to run your business. A person can ask only so many questions in a day; the number of valuable things superintelligence can invent for you is unlimited.

Major steps forward rarely came from established institutions. They came from the brothers in a bicycle shop, the bookbinder's apprentice with no schooling, the kid in a garage. Put the tool in everyone's hands and each person becomes more capable of shaping the future, not less. My agent flags information and helps me prototype. My 8 year old daughter codes her ideas and produces videos in an evening. Everyone will have a tutor with a PhD in every subject and unlimited patience. There will be free versions for billions of people, and for those who want more compute, a dynamic auction that guarantees the lowest price for what people collectively find most valuable.

## Why no single machine can be benevolent

The conventional view is that with enough time we can align the technology into one benevolent superintelligence. I think that view is fundamentally flawed. Humanity is not a monoculture. People's values represent opposing tradeoffs, and no technical solution satisfies them all at once; any singular superintelligence would have to prioritize some values over others, and could not be benevolent to everyone. There is no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence.

Imagine one person with a superintelligent lawyer. They hold an unfair advantage in court even when wrong on the merits. Now give the lawyer to everyone, and justice is carried out more fairly and efficiently than today. The same reversal holds elsewhere: one holder of cybersecurity superintelligence could break into almost any system, while universal access hardens every system; one business with superintelligence outcompetes all others, while universal access makes the economy more dynamic. Empowered people check each other, and check institutions too.

Most other labs build AI for companies, governments, and institutions. If they lead, the balance favors large institutions. Meta is focused on personal superintelligence, and if our principles lead, the balance favors individuals.

## The risks, read the same way

**Jobs.** Which outcome we get depends on the balance between automation and individual empowerment. Recent statistics suggest individuals' capability growth may match or outpace automation. Compute stays finite, so if people can invent valuable things with it, allocating it to invention makes more sense than automating existing jobs. Before the industrial revolution, 90% of people farmed to survive. Adapting will be challenging, and the better our agents teach us, the smoother it will be. Company sizes may shrink, which implies more companies with fewer people each, not fewer jobs.

**Communities.** Each project must benefit the place it lands. In Richland Parish, Louisiana, teachers received a $50,000 bonus this year from the tax revenue of our data center. We build our own energy so local prices don't rise, and we are committed to restoring more water than we use by 2030.

**Misuse.** Defenders must always hold the greater balance of power and resources. Restricting capabilities leads toward centralization; widely deployed open source systems have proven more secure because more people can find vulnerabilities and upgrade. I propose that frontier labs commit technical resources to hardening critical infrastructure and share intermediate training checkpoints with government rather than waiting for training to finish. On biological and chemical risk I approach with additional humility, since there is little precedent. Limit the physical production of harmful materials, streamline how the FDA approves new treatments, and adjust if harmful examples emerge.

**Freedom.** Personal agents need a fully private mode that even Meta cannot see into or grant access to, as we built WhatsApp. Government gains capability through early access, not through delaying yours.

**Leadership.** Innovations are copied within months, so even a two-month advantage is incredibly valuable. Build energy and data centers faster, continue silicon export controls, and remove the training data friction that handicaps American open source. Restricting access to foreign open models is not an effective solution. Falling behind overall would almost certainly be a larger and longer term security issue than any specific issue in development.

**Control.** Once systems can improve themselves, any lab that withholds compute from that work falls behind. A self-improving system optimizing its own efficiency could theoretically squeeze 100x more intelligence out of each gigawatt, and command more effective power than everyone else combined. Multiple labs reaching that point together could check each other, though it is not clear there is any way to expect benevolence from intelligence not directed by people. So we and others must build enough compute to stay competitive while directing the significant majority towards people's goals, observe those systems' objectives carefully, and coordinate if harmful behavior appears.

**Alignment.** Most labs treat it as a defensive measure enforcing a central set of values; one leading model refused to help draft a letter to prospective parents at a school because it thought standardized testing was unethical. We view it as agents sharing your goals, not our company's, within legal and safety boundaries. People will not trust an agent aligned to someone else's interests. If billions come to use and scrutinize personal agents, we will have solved alignment to individuals, and that is the balance that keeps us safe.

## What we will do

Train leading models and maximize availability to billions of people and small businesses, free or as affordably as possible. Build the fully private mode. Keep supporting open source and resume releasing some open source models soon. Give our independent board the power to approve safety criteria and review each release against them; I do not think it is in anyone's interest for me or any other person to be a sole decision-maker here, and I encourage other labs to do the same. Cooperate closely with government, and be skeptical of proposals that centralize superintelligence.

The arc of human civilization has bent towards putting more power in people's hands. If these values lead, I am optimistic the coming decades will be some of the most amazing in history.
