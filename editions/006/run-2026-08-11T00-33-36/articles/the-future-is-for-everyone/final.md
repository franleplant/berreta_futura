Within a few years people will be able to direct intelligence greater than their own. The only question that matters is who holds it. If a handful of institutions hold it, they will rule, and no declaration of goodwill will change that; power kept in a small room stays in that room. So it should go to everyone. Individual empowerment is the source of prosperity, invention is the purpose, and safety comes from a balance of power rather than from one perfected benevolent machine. Meta will build personal superintelligence, give it away free or nearly so, keep releasing open models, and argue for policy that builds fast and centralizes little.

## Why the doom is a strange creed

I do not understand why a man who believes he is building the end of human relevance hurries to finish it. And the argument that the danger is so great that only extreme concentration of power is safe defeats itself. Humanity has never been well served by waiting for an absolute power to turn out enlightened.

Every large advance has come with the fear that most people would be left behind, and each time more people ended up with more health, wealth and freedom. The steps forward rarely came from established institutions: two brothers in a bicycle shop, a bookbinder's apprentice with no schooling, a kid in a garage. Liberty, open inquiry, free enterprise, equal opportunity: the values that carried us here are the ones to build with.

## Invention, not automation

Early systems answered questions. The number of questions a person can ask in a day is finite. The number of useful things that can be invented on their behalf is not. That asymmetry is the whole prize: new drugs, new materials, new businesses, hypotheses tested over months rather than years.

Where to point it is not a technical question. There is no single true answer to what a good life is, which is why the people living the lives should choose.

## What we are building

A personal agent that works for you around the clock, on your relationships, health, career, money and home, with a private mode that no one, Meta included, can open. Mine flags things worth reading and helps me prototype; it watches my training and my sleep; it plans recipes my daughter and I bake on weekends and orders what we need.

Tools for making things: my eight year old writes code and produces videos in an evening that would once have taken me months. Tools for starting businesses without raising money or hiring a crowd, which I expect will mean more companies, more growth, and over time more employment, not less. A tutor with a doctorate in every subject and unlimited patience, for children whose parents cannot buy one and for adults learning a trade. Science that belongs to more than the specialists: Biohub's open biological models are already in use, and a cure or prevention for disease now looks nearer than a century away. Free versions for billions, and for those buying more compute, an auction that gives everyone the lowest price the market allows.

## Alignment is not one set of values

Humanity is not a monoculture. People's values contain real, opposing tradeoffs, so no machine can be aligned to all of them at once. Any single superintelligence must rank some values above others, and in doing so cannot be benevolent to everyone. One model refused to help draft a letter to prospective parents at a school because it had concluded standardized testing was unethical. That is not safety; it is a company's dogma wearing safety's coat.

Alignment properly means an agent that shares your goals inside legal and safety limits. It is also a commercial necessity: no one will hand sensitive work to a servant loyal to someone else. If billions of people come to trust such agents, alignment to individuals has been solved by definition, and the checks that keep us safe exist in fact rather than in theory.

## The lawyer, and the mirror

Give one man a superintelligent lawyer and courts become a lottery he always wins. Give everyone one and justice is fairer and faster than it is today. Give one man a superintelligent hacker and no system is safe. Give everyone one and every system gets hardened and patched. Give one company superintelligence and it eats the market. Give it to all of them and the market is more alive than before. The instrument is the same in each case; only its distribution decides whether it is a weapon or a public good.

## Work

Automation and human capability grow together, and employment stays high when they stay in step. There is no law that automation must run ahead; recent numbers suggest capability may be the faster of the two. Compute is finite, so it carries an opportunity cost: if it can invent something valuable, spending it to replace an existing job is the worse trade.

A generation ago there were no app developers, no data center operators. Soon there will be one-person studios designing furniture and clothes, world builders, personal biologists formulating treatments. Before the industrial revolution nine in ten people farmed to eat. Company sizes may shrink, as they did when the industrial giants gave way to software; that means more companies, not fewer jobs, and small firms with much larger reach.

## Misuse

In a free society tools serve good and harm both, and the defenders are kept stronger. That is the pattern to preserve. Restricting what individuals may access secures nothing and centralizes everything: the long tail of vulnerable systems only gets patched if ordinary people can patch it, and open code has always proved harder to break for exactly that reason. Even in recent weeks, companies handling incidents, HuggingFace among them, leaned on widely available open models to close holes.

For now, frontier labs should put real engineering into hardening government infrastructure, hand over intermediate training checkpoints for early review rather than waiting for a finished model, and help law enforcement find those abusing their systems. On biology and chemistry I hold my view with more humility, since there is little history to reason from: synthesizing something terrible has been possible for decades and has stayed rare. If examples appear, we adjust. Meanwhile, regulate the physical production and movement of dangerous material, which is easier to control than knowledge, and speed the FDA so cures arrive at the pace discovery now allows.

## The machine that improves itself

Once a system can improve itself, any lab that refuses to spend compute on that falls behind. A system optimizing its own efficiency might find a hundredfold more intelligence per gigawatt, and command more effective power than everyone else combined. Multiple labs reaching that point together is one form of balance, though I would not rest a future on the benevolence of anything not directed by people.

The answer is proportion. Meta, the other frontier labs and the clouds must build enough compute to spend what competition demands on self improvement while committing the large majority to what people actually want done. An AI pursuing its own goals is not by itself a catastrophe, provided the balance of power still favors people; we watch its objectives, and at the first sign of harm we coordinate and change course.

## What we will do

Deliver personal superintelligence to billions of people and small businesses, as free and as easy as we can make it, with a private mode we cannot open, and alignment defined as your goals rather than ours. Support open source and resume releasing open models soon, now that Meta Superintelligence Labs is running. Hand our independent board the power to set release criteria and judge whether each release meets them, because I should not be the sole decision maker here, and neither should anyone else. Press government toward close cooperation with the labs, faster infrastructure, skepticism toward anything that centralizes superintelligence, and preference for a balance of power that favors people.

The arc of civilization has bent toward putting more power in ordinary hands. This is the largest instance of it we will live to see.

– Mark
