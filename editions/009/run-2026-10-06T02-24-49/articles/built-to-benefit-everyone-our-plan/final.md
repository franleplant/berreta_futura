AI will soon be capable of extraordinary things, but the point is what people can do with it. Transformative technologies can concentrate power or broaden it. We believe the safer future is one where power is broadly distributed, AI stays safe and subject to human control, and people keep deciding what is worth doing. We have three goals: build an automated AI researcher, accelerate the economy while working to share the gains widely, and give everyone on Earth a personal AGI.

## Light at night

Electricity did not transform every household overnight, and its benefits reached people unevenly. But as access spread, ordinary life changed. Light extended the day, pumps and refrigeration reduced some of the hardest work, and radios brought news from hundreds of miles away. By the end of the 20th century the average lifespan had increased by over 20 years and median inflation-adjusted income had tripled or so. Those gains were driven in no small part by advances in healthcare, sanitation, and living standards, many of them enabled or accelerated by electrification and related technological progress.

This is happening again with AI. It can help someone navigate a medical bill, care for an aging parent, start a small business, or make a scientific discovery. The wonder of light at night probably wore off pretty fast; what people decided to do with it did not. Because technology has been a reliable way to deliver prosperity over time, we think AI should be available to everyone to use as much as they need, where and how they need it.

## Power and control

That future will not happen automatically. Our first commitment is to build AI in service of humanity: to empower people broadly, not see power concentrated among a few companies, governments, or individuals. We believe the safer future is one where power is broadly distributed, so more of the world can help build a resilience ecosystem: the organizations, systems, and people society could put in place to anticipate, withstand, and recover from AI-driven disruptions. The automobile only became broadly beneficial because societies built seatbelts, traffic laws, licenses, and crash testing around it. The goal wasn't to stop people from driving.

We are also clear-eyed about the risks. Powerful systems must remain safe, aligned with human intent, and subject to human control. Entirely automating everything is not the future we want; it would be unfulfilling, and it would be dangerous. As AI systems become more capable, the human role becomes more important: setting direction, making tradeoffs, applying judgment. A key long-term role for people will be deciding what is worth doing.

## Research and coordination

We believe AI doing AI research will become the determining factor of the pace of progress within the next few years. Alignment is itself a hard research problem, and our researchers will need AI systems that help test ideas, find mistakes, and iterate alongside us.

But faster technical progress makes human judgment and public coordination more important, not less. We have long believed there should ultimately be an international organization that helps coordinate leading AI efforts to reduce catastrophic risk. One of its goals should be to make coordinated action possible, including slowing frontier development when needed, so societal resilience, safety, and alignment can keep pace.

## Three goals

**Build an automated AI researcher** that accelerates and increasingly automates research while remaining steerable, accountable, and connected to people. Our internal belief is that by March of 2028 we may have a significant fraction of our research done by AI systems in tandem with our own researchers.

**Accelerate the economy**, through scientific progress, productivity, and growth, while working to ensure the gains are widely shared.

**Give everyone on Earth a personal AGI**, to use in whatever way they choose.

## The third phase

The first phase of OpenAI was research toward AGI. The second began when we became a product company, deploying our systems and learning from how people used them. Now the economy is beginning to reshape around AI, and the central question is how to make advanced AI abundant, affordable, safe, useful, and easy enough for every person and organization to benefit from it. Frontier capability is only part of the job.

Human history shows that concentrated power creates fragility, while widely shared power makes societies more resilient, adaptable, and free. That is why access, safety, privacy, affordability, open ecosystems, and public oversight matter. If we get this right, AI can become a foundation for productivity, creativity, scientific progress, and economic opportunity for the many, and we will achieve our mission: to ensure AGI benefits all of humanity.
