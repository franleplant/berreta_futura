Electricity did not change a rural town overnight, and its benefits reached people unevenly. But as access spread, light extended the day, pumps and refrigeration took away some of the hardest work, and radios brought news and music into the house. AI is that kind of arrival, and we think it should be available to everyone to use as much as they need, where and how they need it. That will not happen automatically. A transformative technology can concentrate power or broaden it, and we believe the safer future is the one where power is broadly distributed.

## What it is for

The point is not the technology. The point is what people can do with it: navigate a medical bill, learn a skill, start a small business, care for an aging parent, make a discovery. By the end of the 20th century the average lifespan had increased by over 20 years and median inflation-adjusted income tripled or so, driven in no small part by advances in healthcare, sanitation, and living standards, many of them enabled or accelerated by widespread electrification and related progress.

Entirely automating everything is not the future we want. It would be unfulfilling, and it would be dangerous. As systems become more capable, the human role becomes more important: setting direction, making tradeoffs, applying judgment. A key long-term role for people will be deciding what is worth doing.

## Three goals

Build an automated AI researcher, steerable and accountable. Our internal belief is that by March of 2028 we may have a significant fraction of our research being done by AI systems in tandem with our own researchers. We believe AI doing AI research will become the determining factor of the pace of progress within the next few years, and alignment is itself a hard research problem, so we will need AIs to iterate alongside us.

Accelerate the economy, while working to ensure the gains are widely shared.

Give everyone on Earth a personal AGI.

## The third phase

The first phase was research toward AGI. The second was becoming a product company. Now the economy is beginning to reshape around AI, and the question is how to make it abundant, affordable, safe, useful, and easy enough for every person and organization to benefit. Frontier capability is only part of the job.

We have long believed there should ultimately be an international organization that helps coordinate leading AI efforts to reduce catastrophic risk, including making it possible to slow frontier development when needed. Commercial and national competitive incentives are hard to escape. By resilience we mean what society could put in place to anticipate, withstand, and recover from AI-driven disruptions, as seatbelts, licenses, and crash testing were built around the automobile: not to stop people from driving, but to make a powerful technology fit for widespread use.
