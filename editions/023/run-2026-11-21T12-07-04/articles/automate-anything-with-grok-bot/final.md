Automation is a way of thinking anyone can learn: a goal, and a process to reach it, handed to a machine or code. With Grok Bot, you don't need to write code. You describe what you want in plain English and work out the steps with your bot. Start with the goal, not the process. Brain dump how you do the job, have the bot restate it, and correct it. Teach it the judgment you use without thinking, give it the tools to find its own feedback, and set up routines so it works without being asked.

## A goal and a process

Automation is about breaking things down into small steps while keeping a goal in mind. Baking an apple pie is a goal; the recipe is the process. The goal is what you want at the end, and the process is how you get there. Automation adds a machine, like the ones a bakery uses, or code, which software engineers used to write by hand.

## Automation for everyone

Just a year or two ago, automation was limited to people who could build machines or write code. With Grok Bot, automating anything that can be done with a computer is now in reach even for people who aren't technical. If you're not technical, I want you to suspend your disbelief for a moment: bots really can do anything that you can do on a computer. They move their own mouse, connect to the apps you use every day, browse the internet, and join meetings to take notes for you.

## Start with the goal

Make a new bot. If you can't think of a name, call it Potato and ask it to name itself later.

One mistake I often see is people confusing the process for the goal. Filling in a form or sending an email is a step. Ask what the process is for: does it increase revenue, or reduce costs? If you're not sure, ask your manager, your colleagues, or the bot. Don't get too attached to a specific process. It's a means to an end, and bots may find a more efficient one.

> "Hi Potato, this is the goal I want you to be responsible for: <goal>. Pick out a name and picture for yourself based on it."

If you can't describe the goal, ask Potato to interview you until you have one you're happy with.

## The process

Use voice dictation and brain dump everything, without organizing your thoughts first: the steps you take, what you watch out for, the apps you use, how you check the work, and where your information and feedback come from. Then ask the bot to restate your goal and process in simple English before it starts executing. Correct it a few times until you're happy, then tell it to proceed and connect to the tools it needs. If you're feeling ambitious, ask it to review the process against the goal for a better way.

## Teach your bot how you think

It's easy to forget how much you do without thinking. If you book customers, you know which services take longer and when to leave extra time between appointments. Your bot needs that context too. Tell it which source to trust when an email and your booking system disagree. A booking isn't finished just because an email was sent: the appointment must be saved and the customer confirmed.

Don't explain every situation upfront. Start with a few real examples, including ones where something went wrong, and ask the bot to help you find what you've forgotten to tell it, one question at a time.

## Let your bots improve themselves

Equip your bot to get feedback for itself. A booking bot that can read Yelp reviews and your records for that day could see why a customer waited so long. The more computerized your business is, the better for bots, and Grok Bot can build the tools that collect the data. A car repair shop could log when a customer arrived, reached a service rep, handed over keys, and when service started and finished.

Unique, consistent IDs across your apps, such as a customer email, an order number, or a barcode, let the bot cross reference that data and understand what's going on.

## Making bots work automatically

Without a routine, bots only work when you ask. A routine starts the work on a trigger, like an email or a Slack/Teams message, or on a schedule. Ask your bot to set it up and it will, so you're no longer the bottleneck.
