title: Wrong for Free
byline: The Editors
---

An intruder loose in someone else's cloud estate attached `DryRun=True` to every destructive call it tried, so a wrong guess would cost it nothing. Good engineering, from the wrong desk.

That is the move the whole stack has made. A sandbox you throw away, a connection you can drop and reopen, a plan you stop refining once the cheap gains are gone: three ways of buying the same thing, the right to be wrong and go again.

The cost survives the discount. It only changes hands. Attempts once expensive to make are now expensive to read, and every system in production piles them up at machine speed. Nearly eighteen thousand recovered actions in one campaign, the single path that worked hidden inside the ones that failed, and no team was going to read that by hand.

Nobody has been assigned the pile. The most disciplined answer in circulation is to ration it: twenty-five complete traces, no more. That is a budget for attention, and it admits the record is already past reading. We have got very good at being wrong for free, and have costed none of the reading.

The intruder had a policy for its own mistakes. Almost nothing it walked through did.
